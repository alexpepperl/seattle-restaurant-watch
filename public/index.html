WHY THIS EXISTS

A repair grant isn’t
a public safety strategy.

Restaurant owners should be serving their neighbors.
Not budgeting for the next break-in.

This project was inspired by The Seattle Times’ story about restaurant owners coming together to make noise ↗. The article’s full text was not available for verification; the incidents here are independently sourced from the linked local reporting.

In Ballard, owners have described stolen equipment, damaged doors and costs that fall below insurance deductibles—or aren’t insured at all. In Alki, a co-owner described three break-ins at adjoining shops and warned that repeat losses aren’t sustainable.

In May 2026, KIRO 7 reported Copine’s planned closure ↗, citing rising costs, slower business and multiple break-ins. Crime was one reported pressure—not an established sole cause. We mark that closure rather than recommend a visit.

THE RECOVERY GAP

Help with repairs.
Not a full recovery.

$5,000current standard repair grant cap

Seattle’s Storefront Repair Fund reimburses eligible storefront repairs. It does not cover stolen goods, and businesses must pay first. Funding and eligibility apply; limited exceptions may exist.

Official policy checked Nov 10, 2026. Older articles cite a $3,000 cap. This is not a calculation of any restaurant’s grant award or uncovered losses.

Feed health & update details

GitHub checks public RSS feeds every six hours (best effort, not guaranteed). This page checks the published files every five minutes. Headlines can be irrelevant or duplicated; a human must verify, date, deduplicate and locate a report before it appears on the map. Social posts are reviewed only through linked reporting; no private content is collected.

Know what you’re looking at.

A small collection, not a crime census

News coverage is uneven. The starting dataset covers Ballard and Alki, not all of Seattle. An empty view means no matching entries here—not no crime. Police-wide burglary totals are not treated as restaurant totals.

Dates, costs & repeat incidents

Approximate incident windows are explicit and overlap date filters. Cross-month uncertain incidents are not assigned an invented chart month. Cost floors sum available lower estimates, exclude unknown costs and upper-only estimates, and are not audited, inflation-adjusted, net of insurance or net of grants. Shared premises are counted once. Repeat counts require two separately mapped incidents at the same location in the selected range.

Reporting standards & corrections

“Sourced” means a public report supports the entry—not independent police confirmation or proof of guilt. Entries distinguish burglary, attempted entry, outdoor theft and vandalism. We do not name suspects or infer links between incidents. Pins are approximate address locations (ArcGIS geocoding / address placement), not crime-scene coordinates.

Suggest a report or request a correction ↗

Include a public source, business address and incident date. Do not post private information, accusations, security vulnerabilities or identifying images of alleged suspects. For an emergency call 911; this is not a police reporting service.
